### 1. Introduction

> *Welcome to **Arcana21**, an immersive roleplaying system that plunges you into a world of adventure, magic, and tactical challenge. Whether you're a fearless warrior, a masterful spellcaster, or a cunning specialist — your fate is in your hands.*
> ***Arcana21** gives you the freedom to create truly unique characters and live out compelling stories in a living, breathing world. At its heart lies the interplay of **combat, magic, and exploration**. Every aspect of the game calls on your ingenuity, planning, and skill — whether you're locked in an epic battle, unravelling ancient mysteries, or navigating the tangled webs of society.*

##### 1.1 The Goal of Arcana21
The goal of the game is to dive into a dynamic story alongside your fellow players, guided by the Game Master. It's not just about slaying monsters or claiming treasure — it's about exploring the world and its mysteries, making moral choices, and shaping the legacy of your character.

##### 1.2 The Core Principles
**Cooperation and Creativity:** At the heart of **Arcana21** is collaborative storytelling. Every player brings their own perspective and creativity to breathe life into the world.

**Flexibility and Individuality:** The system offers a wide array of classes, backgrounds, and abilities, giving you the tools to build your character exactly as you envision them.

**Tactical Depth:** From combat to social encounters — strategic thinking and smart resource management are always in play.

**Magical Variety:** Magic in Arcana21 is not merely a tool; it is a powerful force that shapes the world and reveals its secrets.
In Arcana21, you are not bound by preset spell lists.
Rather than a restrictive menu of options, the system gives you the freedom to craft every spell from scratch — creative, situational, and tailored to your character. Magic follows your ideas, not the other way around.

##### 1.3 System Overview
Arcana21 blends the elegance of a modular attribute system with deep class mechanics and a dynamic combat engine. Every character is defined by three core attributes: **Body**, **Mind**, and **Soul**, each representing distinct facets of your capabilities. The system is designed to give players both tactical freedom and narrative depth.
Whether you dedicate yourself to arcane power as a mighty mage, fight for justice as an unyielding warrior, or resolve conflicts as a charismatic mediator — **Arcana21** invites you to write your own legend. The world is waiting to be discovered and shaped by you.
Prepare yourself for a journey filled with challenge, danger, and boundless possibility.

##### 1.4 Checks and Dice Rolls
In **Arcana21**, you roll dice whenever the outcome of an action is uncertain — whether you're scaling a sheer cliff face, cracking a complicated lock, or spinning a convincing lie.
The Game Master determines when a check is required, which attribute or trait applies, and what the difficulty of the task is.

**The Roll Mechanic:**
1. Roll **3d20** (three twenty-sided dice).
2. Identify the **middle value** (the median).
3. Add the **modifier** of the relevant attribute or trait.
4. Compare the result to the difficulty rating — meet or exceed it, and the check succeeds.

**Special Roll Results:**
- **Critical Roll:**
    When two of the dice show the same number, it's a critical roll.
    - If the third die shows **11–20**, it's a **critical success**: you achieve the best possible outcome for your situation — maximum damage in combat, or the most persuasive performance in a negotiation.
    - If the third die shows **1–10**, it's a **critical failure**: the opposite occurs — your attack misses and you might drop your weapon, or your counterpart suddenly takes a disliking to you.
- **Legendary Success:**
    If **all three dice show the same number**, you achieve a **legendary success**. What exactly happens is up to the Game Master — but when in doubt, it means the impossible: the most spectacular and advantageous outcome you can imagine.

**Special Checks:**
Not every check maps neatly to a single attribute. For more complex or creative situations — such as **intimidation, stealth, or distraction** — the check is interpreted in narrative context. In these cases, players are welcome to suggest to the Game Master which attribute they'd like to use:
- *"I slam my fist on the table to demand respect." → Strength check to intimidate.*
- *"I slip past the guard while my companion kicks up a fuss." → Persuasion, Deception, or Performance check as a distraction.*

This flexibility rewards creative roleplaying and makes every scene feel unique. The Game Master has the final say on which combination makes sense — but a good idea almost always finds its way into the game in Arcana21.

##### 2.1 Start with a Character Concept
Think about who your character is. Are they a courageous warrior, a cunning rogue, or a wise mage? What's their history, what drives them, and what kind of person are they? This initial vision forms the foundation of your hero.

##### 2.2 Choose a Class
Once you have a rough idea, pick the class that fits from the 21 available options. Each class comes with unique abilities that define your character's playstyle. Classes fall into two categories:

- **Combat Classes** — for physical strength, tactics, and martial mastery.
- **Spell Classes** — for magical abilities and the art of spellcasting.

Each class has two distinct variations, reflecting different facets and focuses within the class. They serve as inspiration and guidance, but are not a strict blueprint — you don't have to follow them to the letter.

[[./Classes/-Classes-|Class Overview]]

> [!example]- *Example*
> *You choose the **Hunter** class with the **Ranger** variation — a combat class specialising in precision and agility.*

##### 2.3 Choose a Folk
Your character's Folk describes their culture. It determines your general appearance, cultural identity, and some of your innate abilities. Choose a Folk that speaks to you.

[[./Folk/-Folk-|Folk Overview]]

> [!example]- *Example*
> *Your character is a Lupari (Wolf Folk) who relies heavily on their keen nose. This pairs naturally with the Hunter class.*

##### 2.4 Choose a Background
Your character's background describes their origins and past experiences. It grants additional skills, knowledge, and traits that give your hero greater depth. Choose a background that fits your character's life story.

[[./Backgrounds/-Backgrounds-|Background Overview]]

> [!example]- *Example*
> *Your character grew up as a **Seafarer** and developed a deep understanding of navigation and life on the open water.*

##### 2.5 Distribute Your Core Attributes
Now assign the standard values 2, 1, and -2 to the three core attributes:

- **[[./Attributes/Body/-Body-|Body]]** — for physical capabilities and endurance.
- **[[./Attributes/Mind/-Mind-|Mind]]** — for intellect and magic.
- **[[./Attributes/Soul/-Soul-|Soul]]** — for charisma and inspiration.

These [[./Attributes/-Attributes-|Attributes]] determine how your character performs in different situations and influence the distribution of sub-attributes such as Agility, Perception, and Persuasion.

> [!example]- *Example*
> *You distribute the values as follows:*
>
> *- **Body**: 2*
> *- **Mind**: 1*
> *- **Soul**: -2*
>
> *Your Hunter is physically powerful, but not the most charming person in the room.*

##### 2.6 Spend Your Talent Points
Next, spend your talent points on the sub-attributes of the core attributes to further specialise your skills. You have a number of talent points equal to your Level + 2. These points raise the value of a given attribute and improve your odds in relevant checks. If you are talented in a sub-attribute, you gain a bonus of +(Level) on all checks using that sub-attribute. It is not possible to place more than one talent point in the same sub-attribute.

[[./Attributes/-Attributes-|Attribute Overview]]

> [!example]- Example
> You place 1 talent point on **Agility**, 1 on **Perception**, and your last on **Speed**, giving your Hunter an edge in these areas.

##### 2.7 Spend Your Training Points
With your training points (Mind value + Level + 3) you can gain knowledge in various fields, learn to use specific equipment sets, or pick up new languages. These points let you add an extra layer of individuality to your character.
Every hero starts play already fluent in their Folk's language and the Common Tongue. Additionally, every hero begins proficient with one equipment set of their choice.

> [!example]- Example
> Your Hunter receives 5 training points. You spend them on **Survival/Orienteering**, **Nature Lore/Creatures**, **Medicine/Anatomy**, **Cartographer's Set**, and **Navigation Set** — sharpening their skills as both a seafarer and a ranger.
> They begin play fluent in Lupari and Common, and are proficient with the Hunting Set.

##### 2.8 Determine All Passive Values
Now determine your character's passive values — such as Health, Mana Points, Dodge, Movement Rate, and Willpower — based on your attributes and level. These values define your resilience and performance in play. If any passive value would fall below 0, it is rounded up to 0.

*(Don't forget: heroes may also gain additional attributes from their class, variation, or abilities.)*

- [[./Attributes/Passive/Health|Health]]: 3 + [[./Attributes/Body/Constitution|Constitution]] + (Level × 2)
- [[Rulebook#7.4 Mana Points|Mana Points]]: [[./Attributes/Mind/-Mind-|Mind]] + (Level × 2) *(Spell Classes only)*
- [[./Magical Domains/-Magical Domains-|Magical Domains]]: [[./Attributes/Mind/Knowledge|Knowledge]] + 3 + Level
- [[./Attributes/Passive/Movement|Movement]]: (Folk's base movement rate) + [[./Attributes/Body/Speed|Speed]]
- [[./Attributes/Passive/Dodge|Dodge]]: 10 + [[./Attributes/Body/Speed|Speed]]
- [[./Attributes/Passive/Willpower|Willpower]]: 10 + [[./Attributes/Mind/Focus|Focus]]
- [[Rulebook#7.3 Inspiration Points|Inspiration Points]]: [[./Attributes/Soul/-Soul-|Soul]]
- [[Rulebook#7.2 Stamina Points|Stamina Points]]: [[./Attributes/Body/-Body-|Body]]

> [!example]- Example
> Your Hunter's passive values: **Health**: 3 + 2 (Constitution) + 1 (Level) × 2 = 7 | **Mana Points**: 0 (no Arcana) | **Movement Rate**: 5 + 2 (Speed) = 7 | **Dodge**: 10 + 2 (Speed) = 12 | **Willpower**: 10 + 0 (Focus) = 10

##### 2.9 Choose Your Magical Domains
Determine the magical domains your character is familiar with. For spell classes, this choice is pivotal — it determines what kinds of spells they can cast. But it's no less important for combat classes: even if your hero cannot cast spells, they still speak the language of magic — and know how to read its shapes and effects.

> [!example]- Example
> Your Hunter doesn't cast spells themselves, but has knowledge of the domains Darkness, Senses, Earth, Air, and Water. They can recognise and assess spells from these domains.

##### 2.10 Choose Your Equipment
Finally, gear up your character. Choose weapons, armor, and other items that fit your class and background. Keep in mind that armor can affect your movement rate:

- [[./Equipment/Weapons/-Weapons-|Weapons]] *(Maximum 2 weapons; both cannot have the Heavy property)*
- [[./Equipment/Toolkits/-Toolkits-|Toolkits]] *(Determined by training)*
- [[./Equipment/Armor/-Armor-|Armor]] *(Every hero can start with any armor type they are able to wear)*

> [!example]- Example
> Your Hunter wears light armor, which costs 2 movement points, reducing their movement rate to 5. They carry a bow and a shortsword.

##### 2.11 Be Creative and Have Fun!
Now that your character is built, let your imagination run wild and experience your adventure alongside your friends. Whether you play the heroes or the villains — what matters most is this: have fun!

### 3. The Game Master and the Players

##### 3.1 The Game Master's Role

- **Preparing Scenarios:** The GM designs adventures and crafts an engaging, coherent narrative that keeps the players invested.
- **Non-Player Characters (NPCs):** Creating and portraying NPCs that bring the world to life and react to the players' actions.
- **World-Building:** Creating cities, villages, landscapes, and dungeons. The GM should make the world feel dynamic, living, and believable.
- **Moderation:** Keeping the game running smoothly, ensuring the rules are followed, and making sure every player gets their moment in the spotlight.
- **Final Authority:** When rules or game mechanics are unclear, the GM has the last word.

---

##### 3.2 Building the World

- **Cities and Villages:** Creating places with their own culture, history, and population. Every settlement should have something that sets it apart.
- **Adventures:** Designing quests with clear goals, challenges, and rewards. Players should feel that their actions genuinely shape the world around them.
- **Dungeons:** Crafting dangerous locations full of traps, puzzles, enemies, and valuable loot. Dungeons should spark tension and curiosity in equal measure.
- **Thematic Variety:** The world should offer a broad range of environments — from idyllic countryside to grim and perilous places.

---

##### 3.3 Non-Player Characters (NPCs)

- **Creating NPCs:** Important NPCs should have distinct personalities, goals, and histories. They can be allies, enemies, or neutral parties.
- **Interaction:** NPCs are often the primary source of information and quests. They respond to the players' choices and can evolve based on the group's decisions.
- **Roleplaying:** The GM can give NPCs depth through different voices, mannerisms, and motivations.

---

##### 3.4 Creatures, Monsters, and Enemies

- **Design:** All enemies should fit the themes of the adventure. They can be classic fantasy creatures, bandits, or entirely original monsters.
- **Stats:** In general, the GM can design any enemy to suit the story. For beginners, however, there's a simple method for creating challenging — but not always lethal — opponents:
  Enemies are categorised using a straightforward number system. An enemy of Tier X gains +X on every check and all passive values. A good rule of thumb: a hero at Level X should be able to narrowly defeat a Tier X+1 monster.
    - A standard Tier 2 enemy has 5 Health, 12 Dodge, 12 Willpower, movement rate 5, and +2 on all checks.
    - A Tier 2 enemy mage has access to 5 Magical Domain points and 3 Mana.
- **Roles in Play:** Creatures can act as enemies, allies, or even neutral beings. Their presence should enrich the world and offer meaningful challenges.
- **Dynamism:** Monsters don't have to attack on sight — they can flee, negotiate, or pursue their own agendas.

---

##### 3.5 The Players' Role

- **Character Creation:** Players create characters with backstories, goals, and values. A well-developed character adds to everyone's immersion.
- **Knowing Your Abilities:** Players should have a solid grasp of their character's abilities and stats in order to use them effectively.
- **Teamwork:** Players work together as a group to overcome challenges and drive the adventure forward.
- **Note-Taking:** Players should keep notes on key events, NPCs, and locations to stay on top of the story and build lasting connections to the world.
- **Roleplaying:** Players bring their characters to life through decisions, dialogue, and action.

---

### 4. Combat Rules

##### 4.1 Combat Flow

**4.1.1 Initiative**
- Combat begins as soon as the GM calls it. Actions taken immediately before combat breaks out can meaningfully affect the flow of the fight — good coordination among players is rewarded.
- Every character rolls **Initiative** at the start of combat.
    - Players can choose whether to use **Instinct**, **Experience**, or **Intuition** for their roll — though the GM may call for a specific one.
    - The result of the initiative roll determines the order in which everyone takes their turns.
- When multiple characters from the same side end up taking consecutive turns, they may form an **initiative group**. Within this group, the members decide among themselves who acts in what order. However, only simple coordination is allowed — something like: *"I'll wait until the fighter steps out of the line of fire."* Elaborate tactical planning is not part of this phase.
- If characters from opposing sides tie on initiative, the roll is repeated. Whoever wins gets to decide whether they or their opponent acts first.

**4.1.2 Round Structure**
- Each character normally has **4 Action Points (AP)** per round, which they can spend on actions such as attacks, movement, spells, or other activities.
- Turn order follows initiative, and action points can be spent flexibly during your own turn — and sometimes during an opponent's.

**4.1.3 Repetition Penalty**
- Repeating the same action within the same round costs **+1 additional AP** per repetition.
    - Example: the first attack costs 1 AP; a second attack in the same round costs 2 AP.
    - Opportunity attacks made outside your own turn do not count as a repetition of the attack action — they are handled separately.

**4.1.4 Reactions**
- Characters can spend action points to respond to other characters' actions with a **Reaction**.
- Each reaction costs AP equal to its listed cost and is executed during the opponent's turn (after the triggering action).
- It is not possible to save or carry over AP. When using reactions, you are always spending AP from your next turn.
- AP spent on reactions are unavailable in your next turn.
    - Example: if a character uses 2 AP for reactions, they only have 2 AP available on their next turn.

**4.1.5 Possible Actions**
- **Run / Swim / Fly** — *1 AP*: Use your movement rate to move across the battlefield. *(1 movement = 1 square = 1 metre)*
- **Jump** — *1 AP*: Clear obstacles or close the distance to your target. *(Default: Folk's jump value + Strength | Can be extended with a check)*
- **Stand Up** — *1 AP*: Get back on your feet after being knocked down or falling.
- **Hide** — *1 AP*: Slip into the shadows and disappear from your enemies' sight. *(Stealth check vs. Perception)*
- **Dodge** — *1 AP*: Make yourself harder to hit. *(Penalty on attacks against you for the round)*
- **Retreat** — *1 AP*: Leave an enemy's attack range without triggering an opportunity attack.
- **Grapple** — *1 AP*: Seize a foe and restrict their movement. *(Strength check contest. Reduces target's movement rate to 0)*
- **Pin** — *1 AP*: If a foe is already grappled, attempt to fully immobilise them. *(Strength check contest. Reduces your movement rate to 0)*
- **Break Free** — *1 AP*: Escape a grapple or pin. *(Strength check contest or creative solution)*
- **Shove** — *1 AP*: Knock an enemy off balance or push them away. *(Strength check contest. 1 Strength = ½ square, rounded up)*
- **Help** — *1 AP*: Assist an ally with an upcoming attack check or a check in which you have a talent. *(Bonus on their next check)*
- **Use** — *1 AP*: Interact with an object *(e.g. drink a potion, open a door, draw a weapon, etc.)*
- **Attack** — *1 AP*: Strike a foe physically. *(Attack check)*
- **Cast** — *1 AP*: Use your magic to attack, aid, or alter the environment. *(Arcana check)*
- **Aim** — *1 AP*: Take a moment to line up your attack or spell. *(Bonus on your next attack or spell this round)*
- **Overload** — *1 AP*: Channel Mana into your spell to empower it. *(e.g. deal more damage, hit more targets, expand the area of effect, etc.)*
- **Sustain** — *1 AP*: Keep an active spell going. Maximum one spell at a time. *(Focus check against 10 + 2× damage taken this round)*

- **Free Action** — *0 AP*: Some actions are minor enough that the GM lets them pass as a free action.
- **Speak** — *0 AP*: A player may say a limited amount during their turn. The rule of thumb: one breath.

- **Creative Action**: Arcana21 is built on creativity. Got an idea for a cool action? Ask the GM — they might just let it happen.

**4.1.6 Possible Reactions**
- **Opportunity Attack:** *1 AP.* Strike a foe who leaves your melee range. *(Attack check | Requires a drawn melee weapon)*
- **Parry:** *1 AP.* Apply a penalty to an incoming attack check. *(Requires a drawn weapon with the [[Equipment/Weapons/Weapon Abilitiesry|Parry]] ability and must be declared before the attack.)*
- **Spell Duel:** *1 AP.* Counter an enemy spell. *(See 4.4.2)*

- **Creative Reactions**: Arcana21 is built on creativity. Got an idea for a cool reaction? Ask the GM — they might just let it happen.

**4.1.7 Mortal Peril**
- **Death's Door**: When a hero drops to 0 Health, they enter the Death's Door state. In this critical condition, they suffer one point of Exhaustion each round and are severely limited: they can only spend a single action point per round, and Stamina Points are no longer available to them. At the end of each of their turns, they must make a Constitution check (difficulty: 12 + 2× Health below 0). If they fail, they lose 1 additional Health. This process is handled privately between the player and the GM, so other players cannot metagame the situation.
- **Stabilise**: A hero on Death's Door can attempt to stabilise themselves for 1 AP — through a creative, situation-appropriate action. This requires a check (difficulty: 15 + 2× Health below 0). If an ally is nearby, they can also attempt to stabilise the hero — using a Knowledge check against the same difficulty. Characters with Knowledge in Medicine gain double the bonus. A stabilised hero no longer needs to make Constitution checks at the end of their turn — however, if they take damage again, the stabilisation immediately ends.
- **Death of a Hero**: If a character's Health drops to -3 or lower, they die. Only extremely powerful abilities or spells can bring a fallen hero back from the dead.
- **Death of a Non-Player Character**: An NPC normally has 3 Health. When an NPC drops to 0 Health, they enter Death's Door. If they drop to -1 Health, they die.

**4.1.8 Terrain and Environment**
- **High Ground**: You gain a bonus on attack checks against targets in a lower position.
- **Low Ground**: You suffer a penalty on attack checks against targets in a higher position.
- **Full Cover**: You cannot be targeted as long as you don't move or attack.
- **Half Cover**: Enemies suffer a penalty on attack checks against you.
- **Difficult Terrain**: Your movement rate is halved (rounded down).
- **Slippery Ground**: When moving, you must pass an Agility check or fall prone.
- **Impaired Visibility**: Darkness, fog, etc. restrict line of sight. Stealth checks gain a bonus.
- *(The GM sets the scene and new terrain and environmental features can always emerge.)*

**4.1.9 Tactical Combat Advantages**
- **Flanking**: If you attack a foe who is engaged in melee by an ally, you gain a bonus on your attack check.
- **Surrounding**: When an enemy is threatened in melee from at least two sides simultaneously, **all** attacks against them gain a bonus.
- **Ambush**: If you catch a foe off guard (e.g. through stealth or magic), you gain a bonus on your first attack.

##### 4.2 Damage

**4.2.1 Weapon Damage**
- Each weapon typically deals **1–2 damage** on a normal hit.
- Special weapon properties or abilities can modify this damage.

**4.2.2 Spell Damage**
- A spell's damage depends on the amount of **Mana** used:
    - **No Mana (not Overloaded):** The spell deals 1 damage on a normal hit.
    - **Additional Mana (Overloaded):** Damage can increase by +1 for each Mana point spent.

**4.2.3 Hit Types**
- **Normal Hit:** Deals the weapon's or spell's standard damage.
- **Full Hit:** If the attack roll exceeds the difficulty rating by **5 or more**, the hit deals **+1 additional damage**.
- **Critical Hit:** If the attack roll exceeds the difficulty rating by **10 or more**, the hit deals **+2 additional damage**.

**4.2.4 Damage Types**
- **Physical Damage** — Bodily harm from weapons, strikes, or other forms of raw force.
- **Mental Damage** — Disruption of the mind, caused by magic, fear, or other psychological effects.
- **Elemental Damage** — Fire, ice, lightning, poison, acid, and similar forces of nature or magic that act directly on the body.
- **Special Damage** — Holy, infernal, or necrotic energies that often bypass ordinary defences. This damage typically originates from gods, dark powers, or beings not of this world.

##### 4.3 Weapons

**4.3.1 Weapon Types**
- **Melee Weapons:** Swords, daggers, axes, and so on.
- **Ranged Weapons:** Bows, crossbows, throwing knives, and so on.
- **Magical Weapons:** Require Mana or a spellcasting focus to activate.

**4.3.2 Weapon Properties**
- **Launch Aid:** For stabilisation or extended jumps.
- **Silent:** Suited for discreet attacks.
- **Projectile:** The weapon must be reloaded between shots.
- **Reach:** Can strike enemies at a distance.
- **Heavy:** Must be wielded with two hands.
- **Concealable:** Can be hidden on the body.
- **Throwable:** Can be thrown and then recovered.

**4.3.3 Weapon Abilities**
- **Lacerate:** Deals additional damage to the target.
- **Stun:** Partially incapacitates the target.
- **Penetrate:** Ignores part of the target's armor.
- **Bind:** Restrains and immobilises enemies.
- **Collateral:** Deals bonus damage to nearby targets.
- **Parry:** Allows you to deflect incoming attacks *(Reaction)*.
- **Precise:** Allows you to strike weak points *(increases chance to hit)*.

##### 4.4 Magic in Combat

**4.4.1 Attack Spells**
- Attack spells cost **1 AP** and a defined amount of Mana, depending on their power.
- The effect of the spell depends on the chosen Magical Domain and the amount of Mana spent. *(The GM decides when this isn't clear.)*
- An attack spell has a base damage of 1, which can be increased by 1 for each Mana point spent.

**4.4.2 Spell Duel**
- A spell duel occurs when two opposing spells (e.g. Fire vs. Ice) collide directly.
- Requirements:
    - The defending spell must thematically counter the attacking spell.
      *(Fire vs. Fire ❌ | Water vs. Fire ✅)* The GM decides in ambiguous cases.
    - The defending spell must be an **Overloaded spell**.
- Mechanic:
    - Both characters engage in a **Focus check contest**. They gain +5 for each Mana point spent.
    - **Outcome:**
        - **Defender wins:** The attacking spell fizzles and has no effect.
        - **Attacker wins:** The attacking spell resolves as normal.
        - **Tie:** The defender wins the duel.
- A spell duel costs the defender at least **2 AP**, since it requires an **Overloaded spell**.

### 5. Magic and Spellcasting

##### 5.1 Magical Domains

**5.1.1 Overview:**
Magical domains determine the way magic is used in the game. They represent a character's connection to certain forces, concepts, or elements, and define the shape and style of their spellcasting.

**Elemental**
1. **Earth** — Control over the ground and gravity.
2. **Fire** — Manipulation of flame and heat.
3. **Air** — Control over wind and flight.
4. **Water** — Mastery over liquids and ice.

**Mind**
5. **Communication** — Connection and exchange between beings.
6. **Manipulation** — Influence over emotions, thoughts, and actions.
7. **Mental** — Strengthening the mind, mental attacks and defence.
8. **Dream** — Control and exploration of sleep.

**Body**
9. **Illusion** — Creating deceptions that confuse the senses.
10. **Senses** — Amplifying or altering perception.
11. **Summoning** — Calling forth creatures, objects, or energies.
12. **Transformation** — Reshaping bodies or objects.

**Force**
13. **Darkness** — Control over shadows and the concealment of light.
14. **Light** — Creating and manipulating brightness and radiance.
15. **Time** — Altering or manipulating the flow of time.
16. **Space** — Altering or manipulating space itself.

**Soul**
17. **Curse** — Imposing negative effects or ill fortune.
18. **Healing** — Mending injuries and restoring health.
19. **Protection** — Erecting barriers and defences.
20. **Blessing** — Granting positive effects and enhancements.

**Necromancy**
21. **Necromancy** — Control over the dead and the boundary between life and death.

---

**5.1.2 Spell Types:**
Magic in Arcana21 is versatile and divided into several categories:

- **Attack Spells:** Deal damage to enemies or inflict negative effects.
- **Defence Spells:** Create healing, magical barriers, or protective mechanisms.
- **Support Spells:** Aid your allies or manipulate the environment.

---

##### 5.2 Casting Spells

**5.2.1 How Spellcasting Works**

Spellcasting in **Arcana21** is driven by imagination, focus, and willpower — not rigid formulas.
To cast a spell, the player clearly describes the effect they want to achieve, then makes a relevant Arcana check.
The result of the check determines whether — and how powerfully — the spell succeeds.
A successful roll lets the magic work as intended; a failure may weaken, warp, or outright negate the effect.

> **Example:**
> A mage wants to light a spark to ignite a torch.
> This is a simple effect (Spell Tier 0) and succeeds automatically on a successful Arcana check — no Mana required.
> But if the mage wanted to conjure a lance of fire that strikes multiple enemies, they would need to **Overload** the spell (see below) and spend Mana.

Classic spells like "Fireball" or "Ice Spear" can serve as inspiration, but the emphasis is on **creative, freeform spellcasting** that fits the world and the moment.

---

##### 5.2.2 Mana, Spell Tiers & Overloading

Casting a spell costs **no Mana** as long as it stays at its **base level (Tier 0)**.
Mana only comes into play when the spell is **enhanced or Overloaded** — pushed beyond its normal power.
A character can only cast spells up to the **maximum tier equal to their own level**.
Spells cast without Mana are considered **Tier 0**.
When a spell is empowered beyond its base tier:
- For **each additional tier** by which the spell is raised, **1 Mana** is spent.
- Overloading always costs **1 Action Point (AP)** — regardless of how much Mana is used.

> **Example:**
> A mage wants to boost their Firebolt (normally 1 damage) to deal 2 damage and reach twice as far.
> They spend **2 Mana** (once for +1 damage, once for +10 squares of range) and **1 AP** for the Overload.

---

##### 5.2.3 Spell Combinations

Spells can be combined to produce complex or more powerful effects.
This can happen between multiple spellcasters, or through different magical sources within a single character.
The combination should make narrative and logical sense.

> **Example:**
> *A druid sends roots bursting up from the earth while a fire mage sets them alight.*
> The result is a web of flame that binds enemies and burns them.

The **Game Master** decides whether the combination succeeds, what bonuses or risks arise, and whether additional Mana or AP costs apply.

---

##### 5.2.4 Spell Structure

Every spell in *Arcana21* is built from four core components:

| Aspect | Description | Default Value (Tier 0) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Range** | How far the spell reaches | 10 squares |
| **Effect** | Damage or effect strength | 1 damage or a simple effect |
| **Target** | How many creatures or objects are affected | 1 target |
| **Duration** | How long the effect lasts | 1 round |

These values form the **baseline of every spell**, as long as no Mana is invested.

---

**Focus Channelling — Strength Through Restraint**

A spellcaster can deliberately **limit** one aspect of a spell in order to enhance another.
Sacrificing one component allows for improvement of another.

> **Examples:**
> - **Touch Spell** (Range 0) → +1 damage or stronger effect
> - **Self-Spell** (no change of target) → longer duration or improved effect
> - **Static Effect** (e.g. a magical trap) → greater precision or additional targets

This technique allows for finer control and gives players more freedom to emphasise their character's style.

---

**Mana Expenditure — Overloading Spells**

When a spell is pushed beyond its base tier, Mana can be spent.
Each **Mana point** allows **one targeted enhancement**:

| Mana Spent | Enhancement |
| --- | --- |
| +1 Mana | +1 damage or stronger effect |
| +1 Mana | +1 additional target |
| +1 Mana | +10 squares of range |
| +1 Mana | +1 square of area effect |
| +1 Mana | +1 round of additional duration |

> Overloading always costs **1 AP**, regardless of how much Mana is used.

---

**Full Hits & Critical Successes — Power Without Mana**

When casting a spell, an **Arcana check** determines the outcome.
Exceptionally high rolls can amplify the effect — without spending additional Mana.

| Degree of Success | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Full Hit** (5 above the target) | +1 bonus to effect (e.g. +1 damage) |
| **Critical Success** (10 above the target) | +2 bonus to effect (e.g. +2 damage or +1 damage & enhanced effect) |

These bonuses **supplement**, but do **not replace**, the use of Mana.

---

**Summary — Spell Components & Enhancement**

| Component | Base Value | Enhancement via Mana | Enhancement via Success |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Range** | 10 squares | +10 squares / Mana | — |
| **Effect** | 1 damage / effect | +1 / Mana | Full Hit +1 · Critical +2 |
| **Target** | 1 creature / object | +1 / Mana | — |
| **Duration** | 1 round | +1 / Mana | — |

> These values are intended as **guidelines**, not rigid rules.
> They are there to help the GM and players gauge effects and keep magic flexible.
> As always, the final call belongs to the **Game Master**.

### 6. Character Development

##### 6.1 Levelling Up
In Arcana21, levelling up is more than a number going up — it marks a meaningful milestone in a character's journey. New abilities and knowledge are not acquired out of thin air; they reflect the hero's experiences, challenges, and hard-won victories. A character who suddenly gains expertise in trade and economics might have earned it through dealings with merchants, tense diplomatic negotiations, or losing (and winning) money in the market. Every new ability and every piece of additional knowledge should have a logical, narrative basis in play — one that makes the character's inner and outer growth feel earned and real.

When levelling up, the following values are automatically improved:
1. **Attributes**:
    - **Formula**: Standard Array (2 | 1 | -2) + 1 per level gained
2. **Health**:
    - **Formula**: 3 + Constitution + (Level × 2)
3. **Talent Points**:
    - **Formula**: 2 + Level
4. **Training Points**:
    - **Formula**: Mind value + Level + 3
5. **Mana Points**:
    - **Formula**: Arcana + (Level × 2)
6. **Magical Domain Knowledge**:
    - **Formula**: Knowledge + 3 + Level

##### 6.2 Multiclassing
The ability to combine multiple classes allows characters to develop beyond the limits of their original class. This **multiclassing option** offers flexibility and opens up new avenues for character building — but only when it **makes sense within the story**.

**6.2.1 Trading Regular Abilities:**
Instead of the two abilities a character would normally gain from their primary class upon levelling up, they may choose **one ability of the same tier** from a different class.
That choice must **fit logically within the character's story and development**.

**6.2.2 Restrictions:**
The switch is **only possible in agreement with the Game Master**.
Players should be able to make a compelling case for why their character would gain an ability from another class.
Multiclassing must not be used to exploit **unfair advantages through mismatched combinations** (e.g. abilities that synergise in ways that make no narrative sense).

**6.2.3 Balance:**
Multiclassing allows for a maximum of **one ability per level-up** from another class.
The character remains primarily rooted in their main class.

### 7. Game Mechanics and Key Terms

##### 7.1 Training Points
> ***Training Points** in Arcana21 represent the specialised education and knowledge your character has accumulated throughout their life. They allow you to acquire additional skills and expertise that make your character truly unique and adaptable.*

**Usage:**
Training points can be spent in several ways:

- **Fields of Knowledge**: Gain expertise in categories such as **Nature Lore/Creatures**, **Religion/Myths**, **Medicine/Anatomy**, and more.
- **Equipment Sets**: Become proficient with specific **equipment** and specialised sets such as the **Herbalism Set**, **Thieves' Set**, or **Cooking Set**.
- **Languages**: Learn new languages to communicate with other cultures and peoples.
- **Advanced Training**: If you already have training in a field and acquire it a second time, this can be converted into an additional bonus in that field.

**Calculation:**
The number of training points you start with equals your **Mind value + Level + 3**. These points are flexible and can be distributed across areas based on your character's needs and goals.

##### 7.2 Stamina Points
> ***Stamina Points** in Arcana21 represent the physical reserves a hero can draw on during intense moments of combat or other strenuous activities. They allow the hero to push beyond their normal limits — performing additional actions in a turn when it counts most. Stamina Points can be spent to gain an extra movement or an additional attack, and can be replenished through critical hits or other special circumstances. The base value of Stamina Points is tied to the Body attribute, meaning physically powerful heroes have greater endurance.*

- **Usage:** Stamina Points can be spent to gain 1 additional AP in a round — perhaps for that decisive strike or a tactical repositioning that makes all the difference. Normally, only 1 Stamina Point can be spent per round.
- **Regeneration:** Stamina Points are restored through specific actions, such as landing a critical hit in combat or through special abilities. This allows heroes to dig deep in key moments. Only combat classes can restore Stamina Points without a rest.
- **Calculation:** A character's Stamina Points are determined by the **Body** core attribute. A hero with a higher Body value has more stamina and can perform additional actions more frequently, increasing their flexibility in combat.

##### 7.3 Inspiration Points
> ***Inspiration Points** in Arcana21 represent a hero's ability to motivate and support others through sheer inner strength and presence. Based on the character's Soul value, these points allow the hero to give an ally an edge at just the right moment. Whether in the heat of battle or during a tense negotiation, a hero can spend Inspiration Points to grant a companion advantage on their next roll — a reflection of the deep connection to the soul that lets one person lift another.*

- **Usage:** The hero can spend an Inspiration Point to grant an ally within earshot advantage on their next roll, in or out of combat. Advantage means the check is rolled twice and the better result is used. This must be declared before the roll.
- **Regeneration:** Inspiration Points are restored through actions such as resting or through special abilities, allowing heroes to keep lifting their companions when it matters most.
- **Calculation:** A character's Inspiration Points are determined by the **Soul** core attribute. A hero with a higher Soul value has more inspiration to share and can bolster their companions more often — making them a vital presence both in and out of combat.

##### 7.4 Mana Points
> ***Mana Points** in Arcana21 represent a hero's magical energy, drawn from their deep understanding of arcane forces. Based on the Mind attribute — particularly the Arcana trait — they allow the hero to cast spells, perform rituals, and unleash their magical abilities. Mana Points are the lifeblood of magic, and how you spend them determines the power and frequency of your magical actions.*

- **Usage:** Mana Points are spent to cast powerful spells or enhance magical effects. Each spell requires a certain number of Mana Points depending on its strength or complexity. Heroes can also spend Mana to **Overload** their spells for greater power. The number of Mana Points that can be used in a single spell is limited: unless modified by a special ability, this equals the hero's level. *(e.g. a Level 2 hero can Overload a spell with a maximum of 2 Mana)*
  Only spell classes can use Mana Points.
- **Regeneration:** Mana Points are restored through rest and meditation. A short rest restores a portion of the hero's Mana (normally 1 + Level), while a long rest grants full restoration. Some classes or abilities allow for accelerated or additional Mana recovery.
- **Calculation:** A character's Mana Points are determined by the **Arcana** attribute and the hero's level:
    - **Mana Points = Arcana value + (Level × 2)**
    - A hero with higher Arcana and a higher level has a larger magical reservoir and can cast more — or more powerful — spells.

##### 7.5 Talent Points
> ***Talent Points** (**TP**) in Arcana21 represent the skills and specialised knowledge of your hero. They allow you to sharpen specific traits or abilities, gaining an edge in particular situations. Talent Points are central to the progression system — the way you personalise and specialise your character.*

**Usage:** Talent Points are distributed across the **traits** beneath the three core attributes — Body, Mind, and Soul — to raise their values. Every point invested in a trait increases its value, making you more effective in relevant situations. For example, spending Talent Points on **Agility** improves your stealth rolls and acrobatic manoeuvres.

**Gaining Points:**
- You gain 1 Talent Point with each level-up.
- You begin play with 3 Talent Points.

**Calculation:** The value of a trait is determined by the Talent Points invested and the overarching core attribute. For example, if your Mind value is 2 and you invest 1 Talent Point in **Focus**, the resulting Focus value is 3.
**Growth Through Levels:** With each level, you gain new Talent Points to invest in your traits. Over time, your character climbs ever higher in their areas of expertise — becoming a true master of their craft.

##### 7.6 Combat Class
> *Combat classes are masters of melee and ranged combat who have honed their skills with weapons and tactics to a fine edge. These characters rely on physical strength and sharp reflexes to overpower their enemies.*

- **Opportunity Attack**: Combat classes can make opportunity attacks when enemies leave their melee range.
- **Recover Stamina Points**: By landing critical hits, they can recover spent Stamina Points — giving them more options as a fight wears on.
- **Use Weapon Abilities and Properties**: They can use special weapon abilities and properties such as Parry, Lacerate, or Stun to dominate the battlefield. This generally applies to the weapons chosen during character creation. Characters can familiarise themselves with other weapons later.

##### 7.7 Spell Class
> *Spell classes harness the power of magic to shape reality, defeat their enemies, and support their allies. They draw their strength from Mana and the Magical Domains.*

- **Cast Spells**: Spell classes can cast powerful spells from a range of Magical Domains, whether to overcome their foes or aid their companions.
- **Mana Points**: These classes have access to Mana Points, which fuel their spells. These regenerate during rests or through special abilities.
- **Spell Duel**: Spell classes can engage in direct contests of arcane power — measuring their magical strength against another spellcaster in a Spell Duel.

##### 7.8 Bonus & Penalty
> *Bonuses and Penalties in Arcana21 are mechanics that influence specific checks by modifying the result. They reflect advantageous or disadvantageous circumstances that shape your character's fortunes.*

**Usage:**
- **Bonus:** An advantage that makes an action easier. You gain +3 on your check.
- **Penalty:** A disadvantage that makes an action harder. You suffer -3 on your check.

**Application:**
- A Bonus may be granted by favourable circumstances, masterful training, or outside assistance.
- A Penalty applies when adverse conditions, distractions, or physical or magical impairments are present.
- Generally speaking, Bonuses and Penalties are stackable — multiple instances are added together — unless explicitly stated otherwise.

##### 7.9 Luck Checks
> *Luck Checks come into play when neither a character's abilities nor the Game Master's knowledge can provide a clear answer. When a decision is left to chance, fate has its say — in the form of a simple roll.*

**Usage:**
- The GM or the group decides whether a Luck Check is appropriate.
- A pure check is made (normally with no Bonus, Penalty, or Inspiration):
    - **11 or higher**: Fortune smiled on the character.
    - **10 or lower**: Fate had other plans.

**Application:**
- Luck Checks suit spontaneous decisions, random outcomes, and unforeseeable circumstances — for example, whether someone happens to be in the right place at the right time, whether a forgotten key is still in the lock, or whether the floorboard under an old plank gives way.
- They are normally independent of a character's stats or abilities and reflect pure chance.

##### 7.10 Conditions
> *Conditions describe ongoing effects that can affect a creature over the course of an adventure. They influence checks directly — either positively or negatively — and can be overcome through magic, rest, or time.*

**Core Principles:**
- **Positive Conditions** grant **a Bonus** on specific or all checks.
- **Negative Conditions** impose **a Penalty** on specific or all checks.
- Conditions can be **stackable** — their effects add together.
- Some conditions end **automatically after a set amount of time**; others only through **magic, assistance, or a long rest**.

**Positive Conditions (Examples)**
- **Strengthened:** You feel more powerful and full of energy.
- **Focused:** Your mind is sharp and your concentration is unbroken.
- **Light-footed:** You move with ease, almost weightlessly.

> *Positive conditions usually fade quickly or after your next action, but can also be granted deliberately through magic or special circumstances.*

**Negative Conditions (Examples)**
- **Poisoned:** Your body is weakened by a toxic substance.
- **Frightened:** You are inhibited or intimidated.
- **Blinded:** Your vision is severely impaired.
- **Confused:** Your thoughts are clouded or unstable.
- **Restrained:** Your freedom of movement is limited.
- **Weakened:** Your strength or resilience is reduced.
- **Exhausted:** You have pushed your physical or mental reserves to the breaking point.

> *Negative conditions fade at different rates. Some disappear **automatically after a few rounds or minutes**, while others require **active assistance, magic, or a remedy**.*
> ***Exhaustion** can only be relieved by a **long rest**. It is a symbol of physical, mental, and magical overexertion — something that only time and rest can heal. One point of Exhaustion is relieved per long rest. However, if a creature reaches five points of Exhaustion, they succumb to total collapse and die immediately.*

#### 7.11 Rest
> *Rest allows your character to recover from the hardships of adventuring life. It restores resources and marks a narrative shift between intense sequences of events.*

**Short Rest:**
- A period of at least 24 hours without combat, stress, or strenuous travel — for example, a day in a safe camp or a peaceful town.
- Restore **1 + Level Health, Mana Points, Stamina Points, and Inspiration Points**. Removes one negative condition (excluding Exhaustion).

**Long Rest:**
- A full recovery spanning at least one week in a safe environment with food, shelter, and warmth — for example, at an inn or at home.
- Restore **all Health, Mana Points, Stamina Points, and Inspiration Points**.
- Removes **1 point of Exhaustion**.
- Removes certain negative effects, if permitted by the GM.

**Application:**
- Rest encourages resource management and creates space for character development, conversation, and activities beyond combat.
- During a rest, characters can engage in crafting, research, study, or training — provided the rest is not disrupted by outside forces.
- The GM can adjust rest timescales to fit the world and the story — in dangerous regions, even a short rest is a luxury.

#### 7.12 Downtime

> *Downtime describes the time between adventures, when characters pursue their own goals. It serves recovery, growth, and personal development.*

**Duration:**
- Downtime typically spans **several weeks to months**, depending on the activity and circumstances.
- It takes place in a **safe environment** — such as a city, a home, or a protected refuge.

**Typical Activities:**
- Study and Research:
    - Learn new forms of magic, investigate magical phenomena, or pore over ancient texts.
    - A relevant check (e.g. on **Experience**, **Focus**, or **Arcana**) determines progress.
    - Successful study can unlock **new knowledge, rituals, or specialisations**.
- Training and Crafting:
    - Refine your skills, practise combat techniques, or craft special equipment.
    - A check on **Endurance**, **Agility**, or **Knowledge** determines the outcome.
    - Successes can grant **permanent bonuses, improvements, or new talents**.
- Relationships and Influence:
    - Tend to contacts, gain allies, or establish your own home or network.
    - Progress can bring **social advantages**, **access to resources**, or **NPC support**.
- Recovery and Reflection:
    - Use the time to heal old wounds, process past experiences, or rethink your path.
    - This can remove **conditions** or lead to **new goals and character development**.

**Mechanical Effects:**
- During downtime, **targeted checks** can be made to determine progress.
- The **Game Master** decides on duration, difficulty, and possible outcomes.
- A **successful downtime action** can:
    - improve a **skill** or **talent**,
    - bring **new knowledge** or **contacts**,
    - or grant **narrative advantages** in the next adventure.
- A failed check does not necessarily mean failure — setbacks can reveal new insights or open unexpected paths.

**Application:**
- Downtime serves **character development** and the **interweaving of story and game mechanics**.
- It creates space for recovery, growth, and personal stories between adventures.
- The group can spend their downtime together or apart.
